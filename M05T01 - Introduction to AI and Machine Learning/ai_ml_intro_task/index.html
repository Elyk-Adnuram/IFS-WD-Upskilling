Introduction to AI

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to the ability of computer systems to perform tasks that traditionally require human intelligence. These tasks include understanding natural language, recognizing visual patterns, making automated predictions, and adapting to user context.

AI is a broad field encompassing both deterministic, hand-crafted rule systems and dynamic, data-driven architectures that augment modern software experiences.

What Machine Learning Means

Machine Learning (ML) is a specialized subset of AI. While traditional programming relies on developers hand-coding explicit rules for every edge case, ML systems learn underlying statistical patterns directly from data examples.

In an ML workflow, the system uses training to adjust internal mathematical parameters (such as slopes and intercepts in regression) and subsequently performs inference to produce predictions when given novel, unseen data.

How AI is Used in Web Applications

Modern web development integrates AI to solve concrete problems and reduce friction:

  • Recommendation Engines: Personalizing item feeds, learning modules, or products based on user browsing behavior.
  • Chatbots & Assistive Tools: Handling natural-language queries, customer onboarding, and instant triage.
  • Classification & Triage: Automatically sorting support tickets, filtering spam, and performing sentiment analysis.
  • Content Summarization & Search: Condensing user reviews and enabling semantic search across documentation.

Responsible AI Considerations

Integrating AI introduces ethical, operational, and UX risks that developers must actively mitigate:

  • Accuracy & Hallucination: Models can present incorrect information with high confidence; high-impact domains require human review safeguards.
  • Privacy & Security: Minimize the exposure of sensitive user data to external model APIs and maintain transparent data policies.
  • Bias & Fairness: Training data that mirrors historical or social inequalities will produce biased outcomes.
  • Transparency & User Agency: Clearly indicate when content is machine-generated and provide manual override or feedback options.
